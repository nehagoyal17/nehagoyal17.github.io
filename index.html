The POC Graveyard

Most enterprises don't have an AI tools problem. They have a POC graveyard problem. Teams build hundreds of AI assistants, workflows, and prototypes. Demand is validated. Energy is high. Then almost none of them make it to production — not because the tools failed, but because the approach was wrong from the start.

01
Tool-led, not problem-led

Teams start with a shiny new capability and search for a problem to attach it to. The result is a demo that impresses in a meeting and collects dust afterward.

02
No clear success criteria

Without a baseline — hours saved, decisions accelerated, FTEs freed — there's no moment when a POC becomes a product. It just stays an experiment forever.

03
Learnings don't transfer

One team builds a workflow that works brilliantly. Nobody else knows it exists. The next team builds the same thing from scratch.

04
No owner beyond the demo

The PM moves on. The engineer gets pulled elsewhere. The sponsor loses interest. The work dies without a postmortem.

The fix isn't a better model. It's building the right foundations — and approaching every AI initiative with a framework that survives contact with production. See the framework →
