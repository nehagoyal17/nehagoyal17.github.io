The POC Graveyard

Most enterprises don't have an AI tools problem. They have a POC graveyard problem.

Teams build hundreds of AI assistants, workflows, and prototypes. Demand is validated. Energy is high. Then almost none of them make it to production.

Not because the tools failed. Because the approach was wrong from the start.

Four failure modes — every time:

  • Tool-led, not problem-led. Teams start with a shiny new capability and search for a problem to attach it to. The result is a demo that impresses in a meeting and collects dust afterward.
  • No clear success criteria. Without a baseline — hours saved, decisions accelerated, FTEs freed — there's no moment when a POC becomes a product. It just stays an experiment forever.
  • Learnings don't transfer. One team builds a workflow that works brilliantly. Nobody else knows it exists. The next team builds the same thing from scratch. Copy-pasting LLM outputs is not productivity impact.
  • No owner beyond the demo. The PM who championed the POC moves on. The ML engineer who built it gets pulled to another project. The business sponsor loses interest when results don't appear. The work dies without a postmortem.

The fix isn't a better model. It isn't a vendor evaluation. It's building the right foundations — and approaching every AI initiative with a framework that survives contact with production.

The Enterprise AI Opportunity Realization Framework

I developed this framework across years of building AI and ML platform infrastructure at large enterprises. It has three phases:

Phase 1
Identify

Start with the business problem, not the tool. Map use cases against five AI productivity domains and seven capability categories. Prioritize by business value. Design for reuse so one POC unlocks ten use cases.

Phase 2
Build

Every builder needs something different. Build persona-tiered infrastructure — no-code, low-code, pro-code — across ML and GenAI layers. Build governance in from day one, not as a compliance afterthought at beta.

Phase 3
Scale

Four stages: Research & Viability → POC → Beta → GA. Clear go/no-go criteria at every gate. Platform metrics that connect directly to business outcomes — not just MAUs and API calls.

Six Stories From the Field

Each of these is the framework in action. The platform is invisible in every one. The outcome isn't.

Research & Knowledge · RAG + Enterprise Search
Knowledge retrieval at research scale

A product and design organization was spending 4–5 days per question manually searching 3,000+ past studies, user research documents, and product decisions. The information existed. It was just inaccessible at the speed decisions get made. Built a RAG-based research agent with four specialized capabilities: synthesize evidence, connect patterns across studies, create stakeholder-ready output, answer "what do we know about X?" with citations.

645+ monthly active users. Answers in under 1 hour vs. 5 days. $1.6M/year in recovered time.

Lesson: Knowledge retrieval is the highest-ROI, lowest-risk entry point for enterprise GenAI. The information already exists. The platform just needs to make it findable.

ML Platform · Inference Orchestration + Feature Store
Removing the ML engineering bottleneck

ML teams were rebuilding the same feature engineering pipelines independently. No shared infrastructure. No discoverability. Every new model variant required weeks of manual setup before a single line of model code was written. Built a unified feature platform and a configurable inference orchestration layer with A/B testing, traffic routing, and pipeline-level observability.

75+ models in production. $4.5M annualized GP. 60% QoQ adoption growth. Months → 2–3 weeks.

Lesson: The fastest path to model iteration is not a better model. It's infrastructure that makes model iteration cheap.

Process Acceleration · Low-code Workflow Automation
Enterprise workflow automation at scale

Twenty high-value AI use cases identified across six business functions — each requiring multi-system orchestration, LLM reasoning, and data integration. Each would take months to build individually. Most would never get built. Deployed a low-code workflow automation platform as the enterprise standard with native connectors to every major enterprise system.

1,000+ users onboarded. 1,500+ workflows created. Use cases that needed 3-month engineering cycles now take 4–6 weeks.

Lesson: The bottleneck to AI productivity is not ambition — it's the tooling gap between "I have an idea" and "I can build it without an ML engineer."

Platform Foundation · Evaluation Methodology
Standardizing the vector database layer

Eight teams were independently building embedding stores. Each made different choices. None shared infrastructure. Operational overhead was compounding. Led a 17-vendor evaluation — three-round elimination: product overview, must-have requirements scoring, POC performance benchmarking on 2.2 million real vectors.

17 vendors evaluated → 1 organizational standard. Fragmentation eliminated. Cost benchmarked at $6.4K–$11K/year.

Lesson: Platform standards require the same stakeholder alignment and change management as any product launch. Architecture alone doesn't drive adoption.

Data & Analytics on Demand · NL-to-SQL GenAI Agent
Self-serve analytics for the business

Business users across finance, strategy, and operations were dependent on analyst bandwidth for standard data questions. Insights were delayed by query queues. Strategic decisions were made on stale data. Built a natural language query agent over the data warehouse with a pre-vetted metrics library and causal insight layer.

Analyst handoff eliminated for routine queries. $2M FTE savings. $100M NBV uplift from faster causal insights.

Lesson: The biggest unlock isn't smarter queries — it's removing the human in the middle for the 80% of questions that are already well-defined.

Content & Asset Generation · LLM Access Layer + Prompt Management
GenAI content generation at production scale

A content quality problem that couldn't be solved by hiring. Millions of listings. Inconsistent descriptions. Manual writing didn't scale and never would. Built a GenAI content generation pipeline using the platform's LLM access layer and batch processing. Prompt versioning turned out to be the critical design decision.

Thousands of content items generated. 99.9% success rate. P95 latency under 4 seconds.

Lesson: Prompt versioning is the difference between a content experiment and a content product.

A Word on Where This Comes From

I started my product career at AWS, building developer tools — container migration, .NET modernization, computer vision services, IDE integrations. The job was always the same: take something deeply complex and make it disappear. Make it fast to start, invisible to run, and obviously valuable from day one.

That experience shaped a conviction I've carried into every AI platform decision since: developer experience is not a polish layer. It is the product. If the abstraction is wrong, the capability underneath doesn't matter. Nobody will use it.

When I moved to building ML and AI platform infrastructure at Expedia Group, I applied that same lens — but now I was building the infrastructure that developers and business users would consume, not the tools they'd hold in their hands. The persona matrix, the governance-first design, the obsession with making the platform invisible — all of it traces back to years of watching what happens when infrastructure is built for the builder's convenience instead of the user's reality.
